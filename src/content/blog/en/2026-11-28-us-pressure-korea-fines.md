Korean search interest in "fines" (과징금) jumped on Nov. 28 after a US State Department official criticized Korea's regulators over penalties on Coupang. Here is what the Korean press reported and why it matters beyond one company.

## What the US said

According to Newsis, an unnamed State Department official said on Nov. 27 (US time) that the Korea Fair Trade Commission's "continued attacks" on US companies undermine American prosperity and the potential of the US-Korea alliance. The official added that Washington is continuing to review policy options in response to what it calls discriminatory treatment. No specific measures were disclosed.

## The fines behind it

| When | Regulator | Target | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| June | Personal Information Protection Commission | Coupang (large data leak and related violations) | about 624.7 billion won |
| Nov. 9 | Fair Trade Commission | Woowa Brothers (Baemin) | about 331.1 billion won |
| Nov. 9 | Fair Trade Commission | Coupang Eats | about 300.9 billion won |

The FTC found that the two delivery platforms restricted competition. Newsis says Coupang's fines now add up to nearly 1 trillion won.

Newsis notes that most of Coupang's business is in Korea, but US politicians treat it as an American company.

## Other US moves

- Fox News reported on Nov. 25 that a White House official accused Korea of breaking a promise to treat US tech firms fairly, per Newsis.
- Politico, citing sources, reported that the White House is considering a Section 301 trade investigation into digital trade barriers in Korea, the EU and Australia. That is a report, not a decision.
- Kyunghyang Shinmun reports that 22 House Republicans wrote to US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on Nov. 23, voicing concern about European-style platform rules being considered by Korean regulators and lawmakers.

## The platform bill

The Online Platform Fairness Act is meant to curb abuse of merchants by large platforms, with rules on written contracts, advance notice of contract changes and a ban on abusing a stronger bargaining position. The FTC sent it to the National Assembly in 2021, and it has stalled for years. Only two sessions of the relevant committee remain this year (Nov. 30 and Dec. 7), and passage this year looks uncertain.

## Trade talks add pressure

The paper also reports that on Nov. 25 the USTR proposed a tariff plan for Korea: a base case of 15% including existing tariffs, possibly rising to 25% depending on consultations. The overlap with the platform dispute raises the stakes.

## What Korea says

Korea's FTC posted a rebuttal of related press reports on the government briefing site on Nov. 27, but we could not read its contents, so we do not describe it here. Korean authorities have generally said their enforcement follows domestic law regardless of a company's nationality.

## What to watch

- Whether Washington formally launches a trade investigation
- How platform regulation is handled in tariff talks
- Whether the platform bill clears committee this year

Newsis notes that both governments had agreed to keep the Coupang issue from escalating, yet tensions have risen with each new penalty.
