OpenAI has hit back at Apple in court. According to Korean press reports, which cite Bloomberg and Reuters, OpenAI filed a counterclaim in a California federal court on Nov. 27 (U.S. time). It answers a trade-secret lawsuit Apple filed against OpenAI in July 2026. The case matters in Korea because Korean media are following the AI-device race closely, but it is a global story about who gets to build the next computing gadget.

## Background

Apple's July suit alleged that OpenAI used former Apple employees to obtain technical information about the iPhone and Apple Watch. The defendants reportedly include OpenAI's hardware chief, Tang Tan. OpenAI and the employees deny the allegations.

OpenAI is developing AI devices with Jony Ive, Apple's former design chief. The first product is reportedly a donut-shaped smart speaker. The two companies once worked together to bring ChatGPT to iPhone, iPad and Mac, but relations have soured. One report says about 400 Apple employees have moved to OpenAI.

## What OpenAI alleges

These are OpenAI's claims. A court has not found any of them to be true.

- **Supplier pressure.** OpenAI says Apple pushed suppliers to hand over the names, employers and roles of people working on OpenAI projects. Some suppliers allegedly postponed meetings for fear of losing Apple business, and one manufacturer moved OpenAI work to night shifts to avoid overlapping with Apple work, raising costs.
- **Hiring restrictions.** OpenAI challenges an Apple employment term that reportedly bars hiring Apple staff for a year after they leave. It also argues that Apple's practice of letting staff use personal devices and accounts for work lets Apple keep tabs on former employees, and that Apple's IP agreement covers general confidential information, not just trade secrets.
- **Files taken at Apple's end.** OpenAI claims that a person who left Apple, joined OpenAI and then went back to Apple moved more than 100 confidential files to a personal account. They allegedly included unreleased hardware images, engineering and manufacturing data and supplier information. Reports differ on the exact sequence of that person's job moves.

## What the court has done so far

Digital Daily reports that in October the court signaled it was likely to issue an injunction against OpenAI, and that OpenAI then voluntarily agreed not to use the trade secrets Apple had identified. No report we read includes Apple's response to the counterclaim or any ruling on it.

## Why it matters

- It pits a software company moving into hardware against the dominant smartphone maker.
- California strictly limits non-compete agreements, so how far a company can restrict departing staff may become a central question. This is general background, not a prediction of the outcome.
- Whether a large buyer can steer its suppliers away from a rival touches on competition law.

## What to watch

The case is at an early stage. Look for Apple's reply, any motion to dismiss the counterclaim and the court's schedule. Both sides' accounts remain unproven.
